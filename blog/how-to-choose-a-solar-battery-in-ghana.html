How to Choose a Solar Battery in Ghana

A solar battery stores energy for evening use and power cuts, but the right choice depends on the loads you want to run and how long you need them to run. Use these steps to compare battery options before buying.

Start with the loads you need to back up

List each appliance, its wattage and the number of hours it will run. Separate essential loads such as lights, a router and a refrigerator from appliances that can wait. This gives an installer a realistic daily energy target instead of an oversized guess.

Estimate usable capacity

Battery labels show nominal capacity, but not all of that energy should be used on every cycle. Ask for the recommended depth of discharge and usable kilowatt-hours. Divide the energy needed during an outage by the usable energy per battery, then account for inverter and wiring losses.

Compare chemistry and cycle life

Lithium iron phosphate batteries are commonly chosen for frequent cycling and deeper usable capacity. Gel batteries can suit some backup uses, but their performance depends on charge settings and avoiding repeated deep discharge. Compare warranty terms, cycle-life conditions and replacement support alongside the upfront price.

Check inverter compatibility

Confirm the battery voltage, continuous current, surge limits and battery-management communication match the inverter. Use the manufacturer's approved charge profile and protections. Incorrect settings can shorten battery life or cause the system to stop working.

Plan for heat, ventilation and maintenance

Choose a dry, shaded installation location with the clearances required by the manufacturer. Keep terminals protected and have a qualified installer check cable sizing, fuses and earthing. Ask what monitoring and service are available locally.
