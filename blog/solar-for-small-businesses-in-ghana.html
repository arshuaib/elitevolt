Solar Power for Small Businesses in Ghana

A well-planned solar system can reduce interruptions for a shop, salon or small office. Start by deciding which business activities need power during an outage, then size equipment around measured loads and operating hours.

Record your business energy use

List equipment such as lighting, fans, computers, payment devices, refrigerators and salon tools. Note the wattage on each label and the hours it operates. For motor loads, include starting surge requirements and identify equipment that should not share a small inverter.

Prioritize critical equipment

Separate essential equipment from flexible loads. Keeping lights, a point-of-sale device, communications and selected work equipment running may need a smaller system than backing up every appliance. Clear priorities help control installation costs.

Choose a system that fits the workday

Solar panels produce energy in daylight, while batteries cover evening use and interruptions. Compare expected daytime generation with daily energy consumption, then decide what backup duration is needed. Battery capacity and inverter output solve different problems, so size both.

Plan for safe installation and expansion

A site survey should check roof condition, shade, cable routes, protection devices and earthing. Leave room in the design for future loads only when the inverter, battery and panel system can support that expansion safely.

Review total cost and service

Compare equipment, mounting, wiring, protection, installation and maintenance as one project. Ask about product warranties, commissioning records and who to contact for service. A detailed quote should describe what the system can and cannot power.
